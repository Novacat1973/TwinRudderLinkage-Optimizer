Twin-rudder linkage

For twin rudders steered by one tiller and two push rods. The calculator follows both rudders in three dimensions, including rudder axes canted outward, and finds the tiller joint positions and rudder arm angle that make both blades move together, with the inner rudder turning only slightly further.

How to use it

  1. Enter your boat’s geometry and rudder arm.
  2. Set the targets: tiller sweep, ratio and inner rudder extra.
  3. Choose which values the optimizer may change, then run it.

How it works

Target curves for tiller angle ψ from 0 to ψmax:
δinner = i·ψ + ½·Δ·(ψ/ψmax)²
δouter = i·ψ − ½·Δ·(ψ/ψmax)²
with ratio i and inner rudder extra Δ.

It minimizes the mean squared deviation of both rudders from these curves at 24 tiller angles, plus a penalty wherever the transmission angle drops below the limit. The rod length is reset each time so that both rudders sit at 0° with the tiller centred. Method: Nelder–Mead simplex, started from your current values and from a parallelogram linkage, each with two restarts.

Reference system

Axis point A. Put a plane at right angles to the rudder axis through the centre of the rudder joint; this is the arm plane. A is where the rudder axis passes through it. ΔX and ΔY are measured horizontally from the tiller pivot to A.

If you only know the upper fitting. If the arm plane lies a distance h lower, measured along the axis, A sits h·sin(cant) further outboard. Add that to ΔY. The height itself does not matter.

Heights. Absolute heights of the tiller pivot and the rudder bearings do not matter. Only Δz counts, the height offset between tiller joint and rudder joint. A few centimetres change the ratio by about 1 %.

Transmission angle. The angle between rod and arm, at each rudder and at the tiller. At 90° all of the rod force becomes torque; otherwise the rod force rises with 1/sin of the angle. Below about 40° the linkage gets stiff and sensitive to play; at 0° it locks at dead centre.

Finding the best layout

  1. Measure the geometry

    Axis point A, cant and arm length as described under reference system. For heights, only Δz matters.

  2. Set the tiller sweep

    As far as the tiller can swing without hitting coamings or crew. The tiller end moves sideways by tiller length × sin ψ.

  3. Choose the ratio

    Desired rudder angle divided by tiller sweep. 30 to 35° of rudder and a ratio of 0.9 to 1.0 work well. Tiller force grows linearly with the ratio; smaller ratios need more travel.

  4. Set the inner rudder extra

    0.5 to 2° at full sweep. Estimate: Δ ≈ (2b/l) · sin²β, with half the rudder spacing b, distance l from the hull’s turning point to the rudders and inflow angle β at the stern in a turn. Full car-style Ackermann is too much.

  5. Pick the arm angle

    The rod transmits force best when it meets the arm at right angles with the tiller centred. That happens at an arm angle of about −atan(ΔX / (ΔY · cos cant)), – for your geometry. Enter it as a start value, or set the arm angle to Optimize.

  6. Optimize exactly two values

    Two targets, ratio and inner rudder extra, need two optimized values. If the joint spacing on the tiller is fixed by the design: tiller joint ahead plus arm angle. If the rudder arm must stay as it is: tiller joint ahead plus sideways. With three, the optimizer also picks the solution with the largest transmission angle.

  7. Check beyond the stops

    Pull the slider past the tiller sweep. The linkage must not jam, and the transmission angle should stay above 40°. Set the mechanical stops just beyond the design sweep.

  8. Round and recheck

    Round the result to buildable values, enter them and look at the charts. The arm angle mainly shifts the inner rudder extra; the tiller joint distance ahead of the pivot mainly sets the ratio.

Assumptions: the tiller pivot axis is vertical, the rudder axes have no rake and are canted sideways only, and both sides are mirror images. Rudder angle means rotation of the blade about its own canted axis; with the boat upright this equals the angle of attack. The target for the inner rudder extra grows with the square of the tiller angle, like the oblique inflow at the stern in a turn. Your inputs are stored in this browser.
